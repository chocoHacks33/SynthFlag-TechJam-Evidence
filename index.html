01 / The problem

The internet does not preserve laboratory pixels.

Screenshots, JPEG recompression, blur, resizing, noise, colour edits and crops can erase the visual traces detectors learn. Track 5 asks whether an under-2B-parameter detector can still rank authentic and generated images after those transformations.

The SigLIP So400M vision encoder exposes a 1,152-D pooled feature and fits the budget. It was strong enough to support rapid feature-cached head experiments on an RTX 3060 while leaving most weights frozen.

Data and features prepared

25,000 balanced sources were assembled: 12,500 real and 12,500 generated. Cached views reached 81,378 rows. The final v3 optimizer used an 8,000-source adaptation pool and a separate 10,936-source teacher-preservation stream—not every prepared pixel entered every training run.

Day 3 · guardrails

Reject the shortcut. Diagnose the miss.

The headline was allowed to lose if cross-source behaviour got worse.

Conservative residual-head v3

A 297,729-parameter LayerNorm → 256 → GELU → dropout → scalar-residual head was trained over frozen 1,152-D Expert 4 features. The scalar corrects the upstream two-class margin. Learning rate and weight decay were 1e−4; residual scale was 0.075.

SID false-negative investigation

Nearly all remaining misses were locally tampered images, while fully synthetic SID images were easier. Lowering the threshold rescued positives but rapidly multiplied false positives. Public validation

5,000

5,000

03 / Moderation economics

Why false positives deserve product-level attention.

On a platform, a false positive can wrongly question an authentic creator, trigger appeals, damage trust and interrupt monetisation. That makes low-FPR operating points valuable—but never licenses us to ignore false negatives.

SID profile

Conservative, but misses local edits

Clean: 18 FP and 1,044 FN. The model rarely accuses real SID images, but global pooling misses many locally tampered positives.

Threshold shortcut

Rescue costs rise quickly

Lowering the SID threshold rescued 10 additional FNs only after creating 119 false positives; 100 rescues created 1,566 FPs.

Deployment policy

Tier the review action

Keep probabilities, pick thresholds on deployment-prevalence data, and route consequential cases to provenance checks and humans.

Transparent weakness: augmented WildFake has 861 false positives and specificity 0.6556. SynthFlag therefore cannot claim to globally minimise false positives. It demonstrates why one universal threshold is unsafe.

Selected research · filtered for relevance

Signals fail.
That is the research.

We used the literature to generate testable hypotheses, not to decorate the final score. The strongest lesson was that a detector can rank one dataset brilliantly while learning the wrong reason.

Researcher interview · Day 3

An NUS paper led us back to NUS.

We found “Local Statistics for Generative Image Detection,” co-authored at the NUS School of Computing by then-undergraduate researcher Yung Jer Wong and Professor Teck Khim Ng. Because we were genuinely curious about how its Bayer-pattern hypothesis began—and because the authors were from our own university—we actively sought Professor Ng out rather than treating the citation as decoration.

Compare methods on the same dataset and the same metric. Blur weakens the local statistical difference.

In the recorded conversation, Professor Ng explained that Wong had been his student and teaching assistant, and that he suggested investigating traces created by camera Bayer sampling and demosaicing. The call clarified the mechanism: most sensor locations measure one colour and interpolate the others, creating local smoothness and variance relationships. Their method measures those relationships; it does not reverse demosaicing.

He also warned that blur, post-processing and future generators can weaken or imitate camera traces. That turned the paper from a tempting “extra feature” into a testable roadmap: build a local branch for SID-like edits, compare on identical data and metrics, and reject it if untouched-source performance regresses.

Evidence boundary. The interview informed our diagnosis and future local-statistics branch. It did not train the selected checkpoints, prescribe our head, or prove a universal detector. The current TEST1 graph remained frozen; the conversation changed what we would test next.
01 / Local statistics paper

From author conversation to a falsifiable branch.

Wong & Ng’s NUS research motivated an interpretable complement to global deep features: green-channel high-pass diagonal gradients, 10×10 block variance and local cross-channel Pearson correlations. We document it as external inspiration and future work—not as a component secretly present in TEST1.

Paper method

Spectral percentile

Diagonal and anti-diagonal green-channel gradients are transformed with a DFT; a high-frequency percentile becomes a compact feature.

Paper method

Block variance

Per-channel diagonal and anti-diagonal variance is aggregated over local 10×10 blocks to expose acquisition smoothness.

SynthFlag roadmap

Local–global fusion

Use a separately trained and source-disjoint local branch to catch small tampered regions erased by global pooling. Ship it only if it reduces SID false negatives without breaking the low-false-positive review tier.
